Excel spreadsheets are useful for creating budgets, reports, invoices, schedules, financial records and other types of structured information. However, sharing an editable spreadsheet is not always convenient.

Converting an Excel workbook to PDF creates a fixed document that can be easier to share, print and view across different devices. This guide explains the conversion process and provides practical tips for getting a readable PDF from your spreadsheet.

What Is an Excel to PDF Converter?

An Excel to PDF converter changes a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet into a PDF document. Common Excel formats include XLS and XLSX.

PDF is useful when you want to distribute a document without requiring the recipient to have Microsoft Excel installed. It can also provide a more consistent viewing and printing experience.

The final appearance depends on the original workbook, including its page layout, column widths, fonts, charts, print settings and other spreadsheet elements.

Why Convert Excel Files to PDF?

There are several practical reasons to convert a spreadsheet into PDF format.

  • Easier sharing: PDF files can be shared without requiring the recipient to edit the original spreadsheet.
  • Consistent presentation: A PDF provides a fixed document format that is suitable for reports and finished documents.
  • Printing: PDF documents are commonly used for printing reports, invoices and records.
  • Professional reports: A spreadsheet can be distributed as a finished document rather than an editable workbook.
  • Easy viewing: PDF readers are available on computers, tablets and smartphones.

What Happens to Excel Formatting When Converted?

Excel spreadsheets can contain multiple worksheets, tables, formulas, charts, merged cells and different page layouts. During conversion, these elements are transformed into pages in the resulting PDF.

Simple spreadsheets generally convert cleanly. However, very wide worksheets or complex layouts may require some preparation before conversion.

Before converting an important spreadsheet, review the worksheet layout and make sure important columns, tables and charts fit within the intended page area.

How to Prepare an Excel File Before Conversion

Preparing a spreadsheet before conversion can help produce a cleaner and more readable PDF.

Check Column Widths

Very wide columns can cause a worksheet to spread across multiple pages. Review the layout and adjust unnecessary column widths when possible.

Review Page Breaks

If your spreadsheet contains several pages, check where tables and sections are divided. Avoid splitting important information across pages when possible.

Check Charts and Tables

Make sure charts, tables and important headings are positioned correctly and remain readable at the intended PDF page size.

Keep the Original Workbook

Keep a copy of the original XLS or XLSX file if you expect to make future changes. The PDF is generally better suited to sharing and viewing than continued spreadsheet editing.

Common Excel to PDF Conversion Problems

Wide spreadsheets are split across pages

A worksheet with many columns may not fit comfortably on a standard page. Consider reviewing the layout, reducing unnecessary spacing or using a suitable page orientation before conversion.

Text becomes difficult to read

Trying to fit too much information onto one page can make the resulting PDF difficult to read. A better layout may require multiple pages rather than shrinking everything excessively.

Tables are divided between pages

Large tables can naturally continue onto additional pages. Review the converted PDF and make sure the resulting page breaks still make sense.

The PDF does not look exactly like expected

Complex workbooks can contain formatting, charts, formulas and layout settings that affect the final document. Always review an important converted file before distributing it.
