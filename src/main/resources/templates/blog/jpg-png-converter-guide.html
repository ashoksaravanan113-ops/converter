JPG vs PNG: What Is the Difference?

JPG and PNG are both common image formats, but they use different approaches to storing image information. Understanding their characteristics can help you choose the appropriate format for your needs.

JPG

JPG, also written as JPEG, is commonly used for photographs and other images containing many colors and gradual changes in tone. JPG uses lossy compression, which can reduce file size but may remove some image information.

JPG is often useful when keeping file sizes relatively small is important and transparency is not required.

PNG

PNG is commonly used for graphics, illustrations, screenshots, logos, and images where sharp edges or transparency are important. PNG uses lossless compression for supported image data.

PNG can preserve transparency, which makes it useful for graphics that need to be placed over different backgrounds.

When to Use JPG or PNG

Use JPG for Photographs

JPG is commonly suitable for photographs and detailed images with many colors. Its compression can produce smaller files, although some image information may be lost.

Use PNG for Transparency

PNG is useful when an image needs transparent areas, such as a logo or graphic that will be placed over another background.

Use PNG for Graphics and Screenshots

PNG can be useful for screenshots, illustrations, icons, diagrams, and graphics containing sharp edges or areas of solid color.

Check Platform Requirements
